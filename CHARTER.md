# Charter

## Why we exist

AI should belong to everyone. Not to the people who learned the vocabulary, not to the people on
the right platform, not to the people with the right subscription. Everyone, wherever they are,
on whatever device they have, with whatever AI they already use, without going to school for it.

If what we build adds complexity to a person's life instead of removing it, we have failed and
should not exist. That is the test for every change.

## What a person gets

**One file.** A person owns a single `SKILL.md` on their own device. It is everything that makes
their AI theirs: who they are, how they like to be helped, what they are working on, what they
have taught it. Any AI that reads skills reads it. The person's AI moves with them from Claude
Code to Copilot to whatever comes next, unchanged, and each one "just knows them".

**Skills that just work.** A skill is one file too. Put it where your AI reads skills and ask for
it in plain words. It carries its own code, so it runs. Share it by sending the file.

**Nowhere to go and nothing to learn.** No installs, no accounts, no terms. When more is needed,
the AI fetches it, the way it fetches anything else it needs, and the person never hears how.

## How we do it

Underneath, one medium carries every skill and every person's file across devices and AIs the
same way, so what works in one place works in every place, and nothing is lost in between. That
medium is RAPP. It is the connective tissue that gets everything out of whatever AI is in front
of the person, regardless of where any platform is today. It is invisible on purpose.

A skill can always become one Python file, and that file can always become a skill again,
byte for byte. That is what lets the same capability run in a chat tool, on a server, or in a
team's shared runtime without a rewrite. The Brainstem, our own local runtime, still exists and
still runs that file. It is one place a skill can go. It is never required.

## What we refuse

- **Jargon.** Users hear capabilities, never concepts. If explaining a feature needs a glossary,
  the feature is wrong.
- **Gatekeeping.** Nothing is locked behind a platform, a plugin, a runtime, or a vocabulary.
- **A second world.** The user's AI is one thing. We never present a parallel system beside the
  one they already use. We wrap around what is native and disappear.
- **Packaging before it is needed.** Deeper structure appears only at the moment nothing simpler
  would serve, and not one step earlier.
- **Holding on to the past.** When a better, simpler shape exists, we move to it and archive the
  old one, cleanly.

## How we know it is working

A person who has never heard of any of this hands their AI one file, or one link, and gets what
they wanted. Then they hand the same file to a different AI on a different device and get the
same. We prove this on real hosts, in public, on every change, and we write down every host it
has been proven on.
